THE CHALLENGE

Food insecurity isn’t a single problem. It’s embedded inside a system. Change one element, and the consequences ripple outward, affecting everything else. So any policies aimed at improving food security need to consider the impact on livelihoods, economic stability, natural resources, and political feasibility.

The design challenge with this game was to help make these interconnected trade-offs tangible, creating an emotional connection between the player and the consequences of their choices.

GAME FANTASY

You are The Guardian, a spirit trying to restore the Thirsting Fields. The Ripple isn’t your enemy exactly, but definitely not a friend. A not-enemy “friend”? One that challenges your choices, exposing their consequences on the system.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

By the end of the game, the player will construct an agrifood policy package that demonstrates systems thinking by balancing food security improvements with their effects on livelihoods, the economy, natural resources, and political feasibility.

GAME GOAL

Reduce food insecurity to 15% or less without allowing Livelihoods & Equity, Economic Stability, Natural Resources, or Political Feasibility to fall below 40%. But there’s a catch...you can’t do it at the expense of what sustains the solution: the rest of the system.

GAMEPLAY

To work your restorative magic, you are given six intervention “seeds” to plant and water:

  • 💦 Irrigation Expansion
  • 🌾 Drought-Resistant Crops
  • 🪷 Water Reuse & Recycling
  • 🌺 Smallholder Support
  • 🏛️ Strategic Grain Reserve
  • 🌳 Watershed Protection

Drag a seed into the Living System in the middle of the gameplay space, and it takes root. Add drops of “Growth Energy”. Or strengthen relationships by grafting them together. Once all six seeds are planted, it's Harvest time, when the Ripple tests what you've built.

MOTIVATION

AUTONOMY: The player can choose which seeds to work with and in which order.

COMPETENCE: With each replay, they see their scores growing.

RELATEDNESS: The theme of the game itself is grounded in relatedness and connection with others. Plus, having a “rival”, the Ripple, helps make things less abstract and more personal.

IMMERSION: Narrative improves learning engagement and outcomes. The narrative of the player as the Guardian growing the connections, while The Ripple tests and, if necessary, severs them, caters to our core need for fantasy in learning.

GAME INSPIRATION

Daybreak & Pandemic.

REFLECTION

The FAO's mission is to make agrifood systems more efficient, inclusive, resilient and sustainable not just individually, but system-wide.

🌱 Measure🌍 Real-world indicator🎮 Game proxy✅ Success
🍽️ Food SecurityModerate/severe food insecurityFood insecurity≤15%
🤝 Livelihoods & EquitySmallholder income + equitable accessLivelihood & equity strength≥40%
📈 Economic StabilityFood-price anomalies + shock resilienceEconomic stability≥40%
🌿 Natural ResourcesSustainable agriculture + water efficiency/stressNatural-resource health≥40%
🏛️ Political FeasibilityInstitutional + stakeholder capacityPolitical support≥40%
🌱 Living SystemBalance across interconnected outcomesAll five working togetherFood ≤15% + all supporting roots ≥40%

To model the system, I used their six indicators, and devised game proxies for each, creating a simplified version. I also incorporated dragging each element into the middle of the gameplay space, combining it with others, and watering them. By making each element in the system more tactile and real rather than an abstract concept on the page, the player is provided a tactile, more engaging experience of the agrifood system’s interconnectedness.

At the end, I included a page for the player to reflect on their choices and the consequences. Reflection is proven to improve retention and transfer.
